Social Planning for Hedonistic Altruists

with Debraj Ray

Working paper

PDF

We study the welfare consequences of social planning for citizens who display hedonistic altruism towards the next generation. A social planner must effectively be more patient than its citizens, even if every citizen has the same discount factor, but the planner also exhibit time inconsistency and has systematic future bias. We presume that successive generations of planners cannot bind one another to a course of action. Our main result states that every equilibrium policy with positive consumption that is nondecreasing in initial output must be either fully Pareto optimal, or "consumption inefficient" in the sense of Malinvaud (1953) and Phelps (1962, 1965). Moreover, we provide sufficient conditions for either outcome to occur.

Stochastic Ordering of Random Menus

with Efe Ok

Working paper

PDF

We introduce a notion of first-order stochastic dominance for random feasible sets (= menus) in a suitably ordered alternative space, by using indirect (Bernoulli) utilities. This notion generalizes the classical stochastic ordering of random variables, and enjoys similar characterizations and properties, albeit, now in terms of the Choquet integral instead of the Lebesgue integral. We provide several illustrations that demonstrate how our ordering may be used in economic contexts. In particular, we examine how one may use this ordering to make monotone comparative statics in the context of random budget sets, cores of random cooperative games, choice correspondences on random menus, and ex ante profit maximization. We derive various characterizations and properties of our stochastic ordering.

Rational Planning and Self Consistency in Decision Problems with Anticipatory Emotions

Conditionally Accepted at Games and Economic Behavior

PDF

Belief-dependent preferences capture psychological motivations such as emotions and image concerns. To manage certain emotions, some agents may prefer not to premeditate their behavior, but to remain undecided and act spontaneously. This raises conceptual and analytical challenges. We show that a rational agent never needs to act spontaneously if, at every history, his decision utility does not depend on what he planned to do at past or counterfactual histories. Decision problems with anticipatory emotions are a common class of psychological games where this property holds. To illustrate, we study the effects of savoring and dread on intertemporal choice. We find that behavior with and without commitment only coincides if the agent's emotions are very mild or very intense. Dynamic inconsistency arises for an intermediate range of emotional intensity.
